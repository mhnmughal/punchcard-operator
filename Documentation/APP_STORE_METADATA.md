## Description

Roughly 1,450 characters, well under the 4,000 limit.

```
Punchcard Operator turns programming logic into a hands on puzzle you can actually touch. You are the operator of an old card reading machine, and every level hands you a broken or missing program that only you can fix.

Punch new instructions into blank rows. Patch over mistakes. Load the card into the reader and pull the lever to watch your program run step by step across the floor of the machine. If the carrier lands on the lit tray, the program passes. If it does not, you will see exactly which instruction failed and get another shot.

There are no timers, no ads, and no random chance. Every puzzle is solved through clear thinking, not quick reflexes. A short training program walks you through your first fix in a few minutes, then the game opens into twelve programs split across three control banks: Basic Control, Card Manipulation, and Master Control. Later levels introduce loops, row shifting, card flipping, and dual channel programs that ask you to think several steps ahead.

Punchcard Operator plays entirely offline. There is no account, no internet connection, and no data collected from you at any point. Your progress is saved on your device and stays there.

What you get:
- Twelve handcrafted logic puzzles plus a short training program
- A tactile punch card system: punch, patch, shift, swap, flip, and copy your own instructions
- Clear failure feedback so every attempt teaches you something
- A calm, mechanical control room look with no clutter
- Zero ads, zero in-app purchases, zero internet connection required

If you like puzzles that reward patience over speed, or you have ever wondered what it felt like to program a machine one card at a time, Punchcard Operator is built for you.
```

## Category

- Games Subcategory 2: **Educational** (optional second Games subcategory, Apple allows two)

**Secondary category: Education**

This is a deliberate ASO play, not a default. The game genuinely teaches
sequencing, loops, and instruction based logic through MOVE, REPEAT, SHIFT, FLIP
and the rest, which is a defensible basis for the Education category (the same
reasoning apps like *Human Resource Machine* and *Lightbot* use). It adds real
discoverability, but App Review has discretion here and could ask for
justification if a reviewer feels it reads as "just a puzzle game."

Safe fallback if you'd rather not take that risk: **Secondary: Entertainment**,
which no reviewer will question but adds less to discoverability.
